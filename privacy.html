Stryka — Privacy Policy

Effective date: 10 November 2026 · App: Stryka (formerly Rise) for Android and iPhone · Developer: Buddha Bytes (Manish Singh Bisht), Dubai, United Arab Emirates · Contact: support@buddhabytes.app

Summary

Stryka is a fitness, food and wellness app. Without a Stryka profile, your workouts, food logs and health numbers stay on your phone. If you create a Stryka profile to use Sweat Stories and Groups, the things you share (like posts and comments) and a summary of your activity (for example steps, distance and workouts) are stored on our server so other users can see them. We do not sell your data, we do not show ads, and we do not use advertising or analytics trackers.

Information stored on your phone

To personalise your plan, Stryka saves the following only on your device: your name (optional), age, sex, height, weight and goals; the country you are from or the food style you pick (used only to suggest dishes); workouts and Monk sessions; food and water logs; health numbers you enter or import (for example steps, sleep, heart rate, weight); photos you choose to add (for example progress photos or food photos); and your settings. Unless you create a Stryka profile (see below, including cloud backup), this data is not sent to us.

Timer alerts (for example “Rest over” when your screen is locked) are notifications scheduled on your phone by Stryka. Nothing about them is sent to a server, and you can turn them off in your phone’s notification settings.

Stryka profile, Sweat Stories and Groups (optional)

If you create a Stryka profile, we store on our server (Supabase, hosted in the region we select): an account ID, your username, display name and profile photo; your email address (only if you choose to sign in or back up your profile with email — we use it only to send sign-in codes and important account messages, sent via our email provider Resend); if you choose “Continue with Google”, Google shares your name and email address with us and we store them the same way; posts, photos, short videos (up to 30 seconds, made smaller on your phone before upload), workout summaries, comments, reactions and stories you share; who you follow; groups you create or join and your group activity; notifications and messages sent to you. While you have a profile, Stryka also keeps an activity summary on our server so your profile, badges and group leaderboards stay up to date: daily, weekly and monthly steps and distance; workout, run, ride and Monk session counts; weekly active minutes and weight lifted for the chart on your profile (not shared if you set “See my workouts & stats” to “Only me”); your weekly workout goal (how many days a week you plan to train, if you set one), so people who follow you can see your progress toward it in “Your circle this week”, for example “3 of 4” (also not shown if you set “See my workouts & stats” to “Only me”); personal records and saved routines; and, for groups you join, weekly totals such as points, active minutes, calories burned in workouts, days you hit your water goal, average hours of sleep, the number of days you did a mood check-in (not the mood itself) and your streak. It does not include your age, sex, height, weight, food logs or progress photos. You can set your profile to private in the app. Other Stryka users can see your profile, posts and group activity. Messages are visible only to the people in the conversation. You can delete posts and comments at any time, and delete your whole account in the app.

Your country (optional): your country, if you choose to show it, is visible to other users on your profile, in people lists and on leaderboards (as a flag and country name). It comes from the country you pick in Settings or, if you haven’t picked one, your phone’s region or time zone. Stryka never uses GPS or your city for this. You can turn it off in Privacy → “Show my country on my profile” (on by default for public profiles, off for private ones); when it is off, the country is removed from your public profile. Private profiles never show it to people who don’t follow them.

Cloud backup (only when signed in)

While you are signed in to a Stryka profile, Stryka keeps one compressed backup copy of your app data on our server (Supabase) so you can get it back on a new phone: your plan, goals and body details, workouts, food and water logs, daily health numbers and your settings. It never includes GPS routes or your location: routes stay on your phone unless you share or post them. Photos, your sign-in details and detailed watch readings are not included. The backup is updated at most every 6 hours, only you can read it, and it is deleted with your account. After you sign in on a new phone, Stryka asks before restoring it. If you don’t sign in, nothing is backed up.

Anonymous crash reports

When something goes wrong in the app, Stryka can send a short crash report so we can fix it: the app version, phone platform (Android or iOS), the screen name, the error message with a short technical trace, and the time. Reports carry no account ID, name, email or any of your app data (we strip emails, numbers and IDs from the message), at most 20 a day. You can turn them off in Profile → Privacy & data → Send anonymous crash reports.

Anonymous usage counts (off unless you turn it on)

If you turn on Profile → Privacy → Share anonymous usage stats, Stryka sends one small record per day: the date, phone platform, app version and counts such as how many times you opened the app and how many workouts, runs and food entries you logged that day, plus whether it was your first workout, run or food log and how many days ago you installed the app (capped at 31). It contains no account ID, device ID, name, email, location or health values, and it cannot be linked back to you. We use it only to see which features help people get started. Turn it off at any time in the same place; it is off by default.

Purchases (Stryka Premium)

On iPhone, the current version of Stryka is free and has no in-app purchases; this section applies to the Android app. If you buy Stryka Premium, Google Play or the App Store takes the payment; Stryka never sees or stores your card or bank details. The purchase receipt is processed by our subscription service provider RevenueCat, together with a random app user ID (your Stryka account ID if you are signed in) so your membership works on your other phones and can be restored. Stryka itself only keeps whether Premium is active, which plan it is and when it renews or ends. If we give you free Premium by email, we store that email address, the end date and a short note, and the app only checks whether there is an active gift for your own signed-in, confirmed email. RevenueCat's privacy policy: revenuecat.com/privacy.

Safety: reports, blocks and follow requests

If you report a post, comment, profile, message or group, we store the report (what was reported, the reason you chose and your account ID) so our team can review it. Anything reported by three different people is hidden automatically until a moderator reviews it. Moderators can hide content and suspend accounts that break the community rules; a suspended account can’t post, comment or send messages, and we keep a record of the suspension and its reason. Reports are private: the person you report can’t see who reported them. If you block someone, the block is saved on your phone and they are removed from the people you follow. If your account is private, follow requests you receive and your answers (accept or decline) are stored on our server so the request can complete.

Invites and referrals

If you have a Stryka profile, we store your invite code, and if you joined with a friend’s code, which code you used and whether the invite qualified for a reward (for example, that you completed a first workout within 7 days). To stop reward abuse, the app sends a one-way scrambled (hashed) version of your phone’s device ID; we can’t turn it back into the original ID and we don’t use it for ads or tracking. The invite landing page (buddhabytes.app/r/…) doesn’t use cookies or trackers.

Workout links you share

If you have a Stryka profile and choose to share a workout, Stryka creates a public link (buddhabytes.app/w/…). Anyone with the link can see that workout: its name, minutes, exercises with Stryka’s exercise pictures, your first name, your emoji avatar and your invite code. It never includes your body weight, photos or health numbers. Deleting your account deletes your shared links. 3D replay videos are made on your phone and only leave it if you share them.

Challenges, city clubs and route segments

Friend challenges: if you create or accept a challenge, the people in that challenge can see your username and your total for it (kilometres, workouts, active minutes or steps). Stryka sends that one total to our server when you open the app; only the members of the challenge can see it. Challenges you created are deleted when you delete your account.

City club leaderboard (off unless you turn it on): if you switch it on, your username and this week's run/walk kilometres, workout count and active minutes appear on your city's weekly leaderboard, visible to Stryka users. Your city is chosen from your last location or your phone's time zone (you can change it). Turning it off or changing city deletes your entry.

Route segments: a segment you create is public: its path, name, length and your username. A segment can never start or end within 200 m of where your run started or finished. When a later run of yours passes a segment, the check happens on your phone and only your time and date are uploaded to that segment's leaderboard; your full route is never uploaded for this.

Reminders

Reminders (workout, water, weigh-in, weekly summary) are scheduled as notifications on your phone. They are never sent to or through our server.

Features that use the internet

  • Barcode lookup: when you scan a packaged food, the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts (openfoodfacts.org) to get the nutrition label.
  • Food database lookup: when Stryka cannot find a food in its built-in table, the food name may be sent to the USDA FoodData Central service to get nutrition values.
  • AI food scan and AI coach (when available): if you choose to use these features, the food photo or text you submit is sent securely to our AI service, which passes it to an AI provider (Google Gemini, with Groq or OpenRouter as backups) to identify foods or generate a response. When you ask the AI coach a question, a short summary of your recent activity is included so the answer fits you (for example your goal, recent workouts, average steps and sleep, weight trend and calories); your name, email and account details are not included. We do not keep these photos or texts after the answer is returned. The providers process them under their own privacy policies.
  • Race calendar: Stryka downloads the list of upcoming races from our website (buddhabytes.app/events.json) about once a day. No personal data is sent.
  • Google sign-in (optional): if you tap “Continue with Google”, Google’s account picker opens on your phone (or Google’s sign-in page in your browser) and Google tells us your name and email address. If you already use Stryka without signing in, your existing Stryka account is linked to that Google account. Google’s own privacy policy applies to that page.
  • Sign in with Apple (optional, iPhone): if you tap “Sign in with Apple”, Apple confirms your identity and shares a user ID and, if you allow it, your name and email address (or a private relay address Apple creates). We store them the same way as an email sign-in. Apple’s own privacy policy applies to that sheet.
  • Links you open (for example "Ask Google AI Mode") open in your browser and are governed by that website's policy.

Location

If you use GPS tracking for a run, walk or ride, Stryka uses your location on your phone to measure distance and pace and to draw your route. On Android, while an activity is recording, Stryka keeps using GPS when the screen is locked or you switch to another app, and shows a “Run in progress” notification for as long as it does; it stops when you finish or discard the activity. Stryka does not use your location in the background at any other time. Your routes are saved on your phone. A route only leaves your phone if you share that activity to Sweat Stories with “Show route map on the post” switched on: then the route is stored on our server with the post, and other Stryka users who can see the post can see the map. By default the first and last 200 metres are hidden (a quarter of the route at each end on routes shorter than 800 metres) so your start and finish points are not shown; you can switch off “Hide start and end” before posting. Delete the post to delete the route. The list of popular running places is built into Stryka and does not send your location anywhere. If you share a route as a picture or a 10-second replay video to another app, it can show your route on a street or satellite map of the area (the first and last 200 metres trimmed, or 15 % at each end on routes shorter than 1.6 km, unless you switch off “Hide start and end”) and goes only where you send it.

Journeys. If you choose “Journey” on the Run screen, Stryka records a whole trip the same way (walking, running, cycling and the parts in a bus, metro or car), using Android’s “Physical activity” answer, when allowed, to label each part. A journey is saved on your phone like your other activities. A journey is never used for community routes, leaderboards or your fitness statistics, and is only shared if you post it; a posted journey always hides the first and last 200 metres of the route (a quarter at each end on journeys shorter than 800 metres).

When you tap “Near me” while writing a post, Stryka asks your phone's built-in place-name service (Android's Geocoder, provided by Google on most phones) to turn your position into a place name such as “Al Barsha, Dubai”. Your position is sent to that service only for this lookup. Only the place name is added to your post (you can edit or remove it before posting); if no name is found, the approximate coordinates are added instead. Stryka does not keep your position.

Community routes (off unless you turn it on). In Privacy settings you can choose “Use my runs for community routes”. When it is on and your profile is public, after each GPS run, walk or ride Stryka sends our server the middle part of the route: the first and last 400 metres are removed, so your start and finish points are not sent, and the rest is simplified to about one point every 25 metres. The time of day (hour only), the activity type and the nearest place name (looked up with Photon by komoot) go with it. Your name and username are never shown: when 5 or more different people have run the same route, Stryka shows it to others with counts only (people this month, busiest hour) and tags people add (shade, lit, water…). Your link to a route is deleted when you delete your account; the shape of a route that 5 or more people ran stays as an anonymous community route. Turn the switch off at any time to stop sending new runs.

Maps. Satellite and Hybrid maps, the route planner and some map pictures (for example the route on a map you share) come from MapTiler (MapTiler AG, Switzerland). MapTiler is also the backup for the standard run map: while the OpenFreeMap map is loading, or when it can’t run on your phone. To draw it, your phone downloads map images for the area on screen, so MapTiler receives the map area being viewed (which can be close to where you are), your IP address and the app identifier, as with any map service. Stryka does not send MapTiler your name, account or GPS track; your recorded routes stay on your phone unless you share them. MapTiler’s privacy policy: maptiler.com/privacy-policy.

OpenFreeMap. The standard Colour, Dark and 3D run maps, areas you save with “Download this area”, and the map behind the 10-second replay of an activity come from OpenFreeMap (openfreemap.org, built from OpenStreetMap data). To draw or save them, your phone downloads map data for the area on screen, the area you save, or the area around the replayed route, so OpenFreeMap receives that area (which can be close to where you are), your IP address and the app identifier, as with any map service. Stryka does not send OpenFreeMap your name, account or GPS track. Satellite and Hybrid maps come from MapTiler (above). OpenFreeMap’s terms and privacy: openfreemap.org/tos.

Place search. When you add a location to a new post, Stryka shows nearby places and searches places as you type. For this, your approximate area (rounded to about 100 m) and the words you type are sent to OpenStreetMap-based services: Photon by komoot (photon.komoot.io, Germany), the Overpass API (overpass-api.de, Germany) and, if those are unavailable, MapTiler’s place search. No name, account or GPS track is sent, and only the place you pick is saved with your post.

Route planning. When you draw a route or ask Stryka to suggest one, the points of that route (and, for a suggestion, your starting point) are sent to the free walking-route service of FOSSGIS e.V. (routing.openstreetmap.de and valhalla1.openstreetmap.de, Germany, based on OpenStreetMap data) so the route can follow streets and paths. No name, account or recorded track is sent. FOSSGIS privacy policy: fossgis.de/datenschutzerklaerung.

Weather before a run. Before you start a run, walk or ride, Stryka can show a short heat tip ("38°C now · coolest at 5 am · take water"). For this, your approximate location (latitude and longitude rounded to 2 decimals, about 1 km) is sent to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com, Switzerland), a free weather service, at most once every 30 minutes. No name, account or route is sent. For Insights (“Aerobic engine in the heat”), Stryka also looks up the hourly temperatures of the last 92 days for the start area of your recent GPS runs and walks: only the start point rounded to 1 decimal (about 10 km) is sent, never the run’s date, time or route; the temperature is stored with the run in your Stryka data. Weather data by Open-Meteo.com (CC BY 4.0). When Stryka suggests a loop, the area around it is also sent to the OpenStreetMap Overpass service (overpass-api.de) to find busy roads, so quiet paths can be suggested first; only the map area is sent, no name or account.

Camera and photos

Stryka only uses the camera or your photos when you choose to take or add a photo. Photos stay on your phone, except a profile photo or a photo or short video you choose to post in Sweat Stories or Groups (stored on our server as described above), and a food photo you choose to send to the AI food scan.

Cycle tracking (optional)

If you turn on cycle-aware tips, the period start dates you enter are stored only on your phone. They are never sent to our servers, shared with other people, or written to Health Connect / Apple Health. You can delete them any time (Home → Cycle → Settings).

Apple Health (iPhone, optional)

If you choose to connect Apple Health, Stryka asks for permission to read steps, active calories, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability (HRV), blood oxygen, sleep (including sleep stages), weight, VO₂ max and workouts, and, only if you allow it, to write the estimated calories of workouts you do in Stryka. You choose each category in Apple’s permission sheet and can change it at any time in the Health app → Profile → Apps → Stryka (or Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices → Stryka). Readings from Apple Health are stored on your phone and used only to show your health and activity in Stryka (Pulse, Readiness, sleep, charts and Insights). We never use Apple Health data for advertising or marketing, never sell it, never share it with data brokers, and never store it in iCloud. It leaves your phone only in these cases, each described above: (1) while you are signed in, the daily health numbers are part of your private cloud backup that only you can read; (2) if you have a Stryka profile, step totals and weekly averages may appear on your profile badges and group leaderboards; (3) if you ask the AI coach a question after allowing AI features, a short summary (for example average steps, sleep and resting heart rate) is sent with your question to the AI provider to answer it and is not kept. You can turn off AI features at any time in Profile → AI features.

Health Connect (Android, optional)

If you choose to connect Health Connect, Stryka reads steps, active calories, heart rate, resting heart rate, heart-rate variability, sleep, blood-oxygen (SpO₂), weight, VO₂ max (to show your Fitness level) and exercise sessions (type, duration, distance and calories) from the last 14 days — and, only if you allow “access past data” in Health Connect, older readings, so the month, 3-month and year charts are complete — and shows which app the data came from (for example Samsung Health or Huawei Health through the Health Sync app). If you also turn on “Save my Stryka workouts to Health Connect”, Stryka writes the estimated calories burned in each Stryka workout, and each GPS run, walk or ride as an exercise session (type, start and end time) with its distance. Stryka does not write your route to Health Connect. These readings are stored on your phone and used only to show your health and activity in Stryka. We don’t sell them, use them for advertising, or share them with anyone. If you have a Stryka profile, the activity summary described above may include step totals. You can remove Stryka’s access at any time in Health Connect settings. Stryka’s use of information received from Health Connect follows the Health Connect Permissions policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Sleep estimated from your phone (Android, optional)

If you turn on “Sleep from your phone”, Stryka asks for Android’s “Physical activity” permission and uses Google’s Sleep API (part of Google Play services) to estimate when you fell asleep and woke up, based on your phone’s motion, light and use. Stryka only receives the start and end time of each night. These times are stored on your phone and are not uploaded. Like sleep from a watch, they count towards your weekly average sleep hours, which appear on group leaderboard cards only if you join a group (see “Stryka profile, Sweat Stories and Groups” above). You can turn it off at any time in Stryka (Devices & apps) or remove the permission in Android settings. Sleep from a watch or that you type in always takes priority.

Steps counted by your phone (Android, optional)

If you turn on “Steps from your phone”, Stryka uses your phone’s built-in step sensor (with Android’s “Physical activity” permission) to count your steps, about every 15 minutes, even when Stryka is closed. Daily step totals are stored on your phone. Like steps from Health Connect, they count towards the step totals shown on your profile badges and on group leaderboards if you have a Stryka profile or join a group. Steps from Health Connect, a watch or that you type in always take priority. You can turn it off at any time in Stryka (Devices & apps).

Detect walks and runs (Android, optional)

If you turn on ‘Detect walks and runs’ (Profile → Settings, off by default; it asks for Android’s “Physical activity” permission), Stryka uses Google Play services’ activity recognition and your phone’s step counter to notice when a walk, run or bike ride starts and stops. Walks, runs and rides of 10 minutes or more are logged in Stryka without GPS (start and end time, steps, and an estimated distance and calories), shown as “Auto · Walk 18 min”; you can edit, delete or post each one, and switch off “Log them automatically” at any time. An auto entry is not added when a GPS activity, a workout or a Health Connect / watch session already covers that time, and it never adds to your daily step, active-minute or calorie totals. If you keep “Ask ‘Track with GPS?’” on, Stryka can also show one notification (“Looks like you’re walking · Track with GPS?”); no location is used until you tap Track with GPS. When detection is on, Stryka turns it back on after your phone restarts or the app updates. The motion readings and step counts themselves are not sent to our server; the auto entries are kept with your other workouts (so they are part of the cloud backup while you are signed in, see “Cloud backup”) and are only shown to others if you post one. Google Play services on your phone tells Stryka when walking or running starts and stops; this, and the step count used to estimate the minutes before you tapped Track with GPS, stays on your phone and is not sent to our server. While you record a GPS run, walk or ride, Stryka also asks Google Play services whether you are on foot, on a bicycle or in a vehicle (with the same “Physical activity” permission, asked once when you first press Start), so a bus or car part isn’t counted as running or riding. This answer is used only during the recording, stays on your phone and is not sent to our server.

Health information

Health scores, calorie estimates, biological age and similar numbers in Stryka are estimates for general wellness only. Stryka is not a medical device and does not give medical advice. Talk to a doctor before starting a new exercise or diet plan, especially if you have a health condition.

Sharing

We do not sell, rent or share your personal information. Data only leaves your phone for the specific features listed above, and only when you use them.

Sharing to other apps. When you share a picture or a 10-second replay video to another app (for example Instagram or WhatsApp) or save it (Documents/Stryka; on older Android versions you choose where in your phone’s share menu), Stryka makes it on your phone and passes it on only when you tap Share or Save; it is not sent to our servers. What happens next is covered by that app’s privacy policy.

Children

Stryka is intended for adults and is not directed at anyone under 18. We do not knowingly collect information from children.

Deleting your data

You can delete everything at any time in the app: Profile → Delete account (or Delete all my data if you never created a profile). This permanently deletes your account and everything you shared from our server, and all Stryka data on your phone. See also our account deletion page.

Security

Data is stored in the app's private storage on your phone. Internet requests made by Stryka use encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Future versions

Future updates may add more features that use the internet. If they do, we will update this policy first.
